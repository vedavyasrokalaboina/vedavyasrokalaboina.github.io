MSc Dissertation

Bio-Inspired Blended Wing Body Aircraft

Apr 2025 – Sep 2025 ANSYS Fluent · k-omega SST <1% validated deviation
Overview

For my MSc dissertation, I designed and optimised a bio-inspired Blended Wing Body (BWB) aircraft configuration, where the wing and fuselage merge into a single continuous aerodynamic surface rather than meeting at a hard junction — reducing interference drag and improving lift distribution across the whole aircraft.

The initial geometry was inspired by the stingray's natural blended form, and modelled in SolidWorks to achieve smooth aerodynamic contours and balanced lift generation, with particular attention paid to how the winglets integrated with the central body.

Winglet Sweep Study

With the framework validated, I ran a parametric study across several winglet sweep angles, testing each under identical flow conditions to evaluate lift generation, drag, and overall aerodynamic efficiency.

Result

A 38° sweep came out clearly ahead, producing the highest lift-to-drag ratio of every configuration tested. The results confirmed the aerodynamic advantages of the Blended Wing Body layout — meaningfully reduced drag and improved lift efficiency compared with the alternatives, the kind of result that makes the case for why configurations like this keep coming up in conversations about the next generation of more fuel-efficient aircraft.
